# FarmPro Management: Architecture and Implementation Specification

**Status:** Proposed v1; planning only. No database objects or migrations have been created.

## 1. Scope and design decisions

This specification defines a practical first release for Dashboard, Farm Management, Livestock, Poultry, Inventory, Production, Health, Breeding, Sales and Customers, Purchases and Suppliers, Expenses and Finance, Tasks and Activities, and Reports.

The design follows these decisions:

- A farm has one owner in v1. Multi-user membership, roles, and invitations are excluded until shared access is a confirmed requirement.
- A user may own more than one farm. Every operational record belongs to exactly one farm.
- Individually identified mammals are stored as animals. Poultry is managed in batches. “Other” animal species can use the livestock record.
- Purchases record received inventory, not purchase orders. There is no unfulfilled-order workflow.
- Sales may record inventory products, individual animals, or a number of birds from a poultry batch.
- Inventory, flock counts, document totals, payment balances, and animal status are derived from source records, not manually maintained totals.
- Approved v1 scope excludes livestock `acquisition_type`, `purchase_cost`, and livestock acquisition-cost accounting. Animal and poultry records retain `acquired_on` for husbandry, but purchases of livestock, their acquisition outflows, and related supplier payables are not represented in finance reports.
- Money uses the farm's currency and is stored to two decimal places. Currency conversion and tax accounting are out of scope.
- Quantities use the item's single base unit. Unit conversion is not supported in v1.
- Date-only operational events use `date`; audit and creation times use `timestamptz`.
- Use PostgreSQL `uuid`, `text`, `date`, `timestamptz`, `boolean`, and `numeric` types. Use `text` plus `CHECK` constraints for finite choices rather than creating many custom enum types.
- All IDs are generated with `gen_random_uuid()`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are generated by the database.

No separate tables are proposed for addresses, animal species, units, categories, locations, attachments, audit logs, notifications, or report snapshots. Those add complexity without a demonstrated v1 workflow. Optional text address/contact fields and constrained values are sufficient.

## 2. Database design

### 2.1 Common conventions

Every farm-owned table has:

- `id uuid`: required, system-generated primary key.
- `farm_id uuid`: required, system-associated foreign key to `farms.id`.

Every operational table has `farm_id NOT NULL REFERENCES farms(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`, `PRIMARY KEY (id)`, and `UNIQUE (farm_id,id)`. Every relationship from one farm-owned row to another uses a composite foreign key `(farm_id, related_id)` referencing `(farm_id,id)` with `ON DELETE RESTRICT`. This makes cross-farm links impossible even if application validation fails. The exceptions are global `profiles` (whose primary key references `auth.users`) and `farms` (whose `owner_id` references `auth.users`). Do not cascade-delete operational history.

Add `created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()` to user-created records except `purchase_lines` and `sale_lines`: they have no independent lifecycle or audit purpose, and their parent document's `created_at` timestamps the transaction. Add `updated_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()` only to editable master records (`farms`, `counterparties`, `animals`, `inventory_items`), maintained by a database trigger. Event/transaction rows do not need a redundant `updated_at`.

All enumerated values and conditional rules below are database `CHECK` constraints unless explicitly identified as requiring a trigger or transaction function. Every listed relationship is a composite same-farm foreign key unless it points to `auth.users` or `farms`. Indexes are listed with each table; each foreign-key column set used for joins/deletes must have a supporting index, combined with the farm key where appropriate.

`numeric(12,3)` is used for quantities; `numeric(12,2)` for money. Amounts and positive quantities must be greater than zero unless a signed adjustment is explicitly described.

#### `inventory_items`

Purpose: define farm-stocked inputs and saleable products with one stock unit per item.

| Column | Type | Required / origin / default | Keys and rules | Form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | `uuid` | Required; system | PK; unique `(farm_id,id)` | Automatic |
| `farm_id` | `uuid` | Required; system | FK to farm | Automatic |
| `name` | `text` | Required; user-entered | Trimmed, 1–120 chars; unique per farm ignoring case | Inventory item form |
| `category` | `text` | Required; user-entered | `feed`, `medicine`, `supplies`, `product`, `other` | Inventory item form |
| `unit` | `text` | Required; user-entered | `kg`, `g`, `l`, `ml`, `unit`, `bag`, `bale`, `tray`, `dozen` | Inventory item form |
| `reorder_level` | `numeric(12,3)` | Optional; user-entered | Greater than or equal to zero; same unit as item | Inventory item form |
| `is_active` | `boolean` | Required; user-managed; default `true` | Inactive items are unavailable in new operational forms; history remains visible | Inventory item form / retire-item action |
| `created_at` | `timestamptz` | Required; system | `now()` | Automatic |
| `updated_at` | `timestamptz` | Required; system | Trigger maintained | Automatic |

Checks constrain category, unit, and non-negative reorder level. A unique expression index on `(farm_id, lower(name))` prevents duplicate names ignoring case. Modules: Inventory, Purchases, Production, Health, Sales, Dashboard, Reports. Index `(farm_id,category,name)` and the unique name index. The UI shows stock on hand from the inventory balance view. No editable quantity-on-hand field.

Name and reorder level may be edited at any time. Category and unit may be changed only while no transaction/history row references the item; enforce this with a database trigger. Reference-validation triggers lock the item row before validating category/unit and active state, preventing concurrent reclassification and use. Database triggers also reject new stock, production, health, or sale references to inactive items. `is_active` exists solely to support safe retirement: an item can be retired only at zero stock, and once it has history a retired item cannot be reactivated. The correction procedure is in Business Rules.

### 2.4 Views and calculated values (not tables)

Implement read-only SQL views with PostgreSQL security-invoker behavior (`WITH (security_invoker = true)`) so queries run with the caller's privileges and preserve underlying-table RLS. Confirm the deployed Supabase PostgreSQL version supports this option before implementation; otherwise use an equally safe, explicitly tested approach that enforces the same `auth.uid()`/farm ownership checks. Never rely on default owner-privileged view behavior.

- `inventory_balances`: build a `movement_rows` CTE with `UNION ALL` rows of `(farm_id,item_id,quantity_delta)` from purchase lines (`+quantity`), production records (`+quantity`), inventory adjustments (`quantity_change`), inventory consumptions (`-quantity`), and product sale lines (`-quantity`, only where `item_id IS NOT NULL`). Aggregate `SUM(quantity_delta)` grouped by `farm_id,item_id`, then left join that aggregate to all `inventory_items` and `COALESCE` missing sums to zero. This preserves one row for every item, including zero-balance items. Do not add a mutable balance column.
- `poultry_batch_balances`: aggregate `poultry_batch_events.quantity_change` per farm/batch and batch sale-line quantities per farm/batch separately, then left join each aggregate to all `poultry_batches`; compute `initial_count + event_delta - sold_count`, coalescing absent aggregates to zero. This avoids join fan-out and keeps zero-count batches visible.
- `animal_status`: `dead` if a death health record exists, else `sold` if a sale line exists, else `active`.
- `sale_totals`: sum of sale line quantity × unit price.
- `purchase_totals`: sum of purchase line quantity × unit cost.
- `payment_balances`: compute each sale, purchase, and expense total and each target's payment sum in separate per-target aggregates, then join and subtract. These are current balances across all dates, not period-filtered. Never join raw lines and payments together before aggregation, which would multiply totals.

All views must expose `farm_id`; do not use a view or RPC that bypasses farm RLS. Dashboard and reports query these views and period-filtered source tables. Do not persist dashboard totals.

## 3. Forms and module workflows

Fields marked “automatic” are never typed by a user. “Calculated” values are displayed read-only. Forms should save required data only; optional fields must not block submission.

### Dashboard

- **Farm selector** (only if the user has multiple farms): required selection; farm name from `farms`.
- **Overview cards**: calculated sales recorded, purchases recorded, operating expenses recorded, cash received, cash paid, current receivables, current payables, low-stock item count, active animal count, current poultry count, and open/overdue tasks.
- **Date range**: optional filter; defaults to current month. Sales use `sales.sold_on`, purchases use `purchases.purchased_on`, expenses use `expenses.spent_on`, and cash received/paid use `payments.paid_on`. Receivables and payables are current outstanding balances, not period-filtered. Each card links to its source list. No dashboard editing form.

### Farm Management

- **Create farm**: required name, currency, timezone; automatic owner, ID, timestamps.
- **Edit farm**: same user-managed fields; owner is immutable. No speculative farm address/size/production fields.
- **Contacts**: required role and name; optional phone, email, address. Create once, then select in sales, purchases, or expenses.
- **Edit contact role**: the form disables role choices that would invalidate existing document references and shows which customer/supplier records block the change. Adding the missing role (selecting `both`) remains available.
- **Account profile**: optional display name; email/password handled by Supabase Auth UI.

### Livestock

- **Add/edit animal**: required species and acquired date; optional tag, name, sex, birth date, dam, sire. Parent selectors are same-species farm animals where practical, with “unknown/not recorded” available. ID, farm, timestamps automatic; status calculated. V1 excludes livestock acquisition type, purchase cost, and acquisition-cost accounting.
- **Animal detail**: read-only derived status and history; links to health, breeding, production, consumption, and sales.
- **Mark death**: create a health record with event type `death`, date, required description; animal status then derives as dead. Do not expose a status dropdown.
- **Record animal sale**: use sale form and select one or more eligible animals; sale flow rejects dead/already-sold animals.

### Poultry

- **Create batch**: required species, purpose, acquired date, initial count; optional batch name.
- **Edit batch**: batch details may be edited as permitted, but `initial_count` is editable only until the first count event or bird sale. After that, show the starting count as read-only and direct count corrections to an adjustment event.
- **Record batch event**: required batch, event type, date, signed count change; adjustment requires reason. UI asks for a positive number of birds and converts deaths to a negative stored change.
- **Batch detail**: current count is read-only and calculated; production, health, consumption, and sale history are linked.

### Inventory

- **Create/edit item**: required name, category, base unit; optional reorder level; `is_active` is created as true and managed through a separate retire action, not ordinary free-form editing. Name and reorder level remain editable. Category and base unit become read-only after any transaction/history row references the item. Item retirement is allowed only at zero stock; inactive items remain visible in history and cannot be selected in new operational forms.
- **Record consumption**: required item, quantity, date; optional animal OR batch, notes. Available quantity is shown. Saving is rejected atomically if it would make stock negative. Medicine administered as part of treatment is recorded only through the combined treatment-with-medicine workflow; do not make a second standalone medicine-consumption entry for that treatment.
- **Adjust stock**: required item, signed quantity change, date, reason. Display current stock and projected stock before confirmation; cannot reduce below zero.
- **Stock list/detail**: on-hand quantity and reorder warning are calculated. Receipts, consumption, production, sales, and adjustments form a read-only movement history assembled from source records.

### Production

- **Record production**: required product item, quantity, date, and exactly one source animal or poultry batch; optional notes. Product selector only shows `product` items. Saving increases item stock.
- **Production history**: filters by date, item, animal, or batch; totals are calculated from records.

### Health

- **Record health event or treatment without stock use**: required animal OR poultry batch, event type, date, description; optional notes. Non-treatment events use the ordinary health-record workflow. A treatment without inventory consumption has no medicine item, creates no stock movement, and still submits through `record_treatment` with its request UUID.
- **Record treatment with medicine consumption**: require the animal OR batch, treatment date and description, a medicine-category item, and quantity administered in that item's base unit; notes remain optional. Submit once to an atomic database function that creates the treatment health record and exactly one linked inventory-consumption row. The UI must not then offer a second consumption submission for that same treatment.
- Medicine quantity is stored only on the linked inventory-consumption row. A health record alone never deducts stock, and inventory calculations count each linked consumption row once. The database enforces same farm, treatment type, medicine item, source animal/batch, date, and at most one linked consumption per health record; it does not use medicine/source/date as a duplicate key.

### Breeding

- **Record breeding event**: required dam, event type, date; optional sire, result, expected due date, notes. Result choices depend on event type. Candidate dam/sire sex checks are advisory if sex is unknown and enforced if known.
- **Record offspring**: use Add Animal with dam/sire selected. No separate offspring-count field.

### Sales and Customers

- **Create/edit customer**: contact form; role is customer or both.
- **Record sale**: required sale date and at least one line; optional customer, reference, notes. Each line is exactly one of:
  - product item + quantity + unit price;
  - active individual animal + unit price (quantity is automatically 1); or
  - poultry batch + whole bird count + per-bird unit price.
- **Record receipt**: select sale, enter amount/date; optional method/reference. Show remaining balance; overpayment rejected by database.
- **Sale detail**: line totals, sale total, payments, and balance are calculated. A sale and its lines save as one transaction.
- A sale line can select an individual animal only while its derived status is active. The database enforces at most one sale for that animal and rejects sale/death conflicts.

### Purchases and Suppliers

- **Create/edit supplier**: contact form; role is supplier or both.
- **Record received purchase**: required date and at least one line; optional supplier, invoice reference, notes. Each line requires stock item, positive quantity, and non-negative unit cost. Receipt increases stock when the transaction commits.
- **Record supplier payment**: select purchase, enter amount/date; optional method/reference. Show remaining balance; overpayment rejected by database.
- **Purchase detail**: line totals, purchase total, payments, balance are calculated. No purchase-order or partial-receipt workflow in v1.

### Expenses and Finance

- **Record expense**: required category, positive amount, date, description; optional supplier.
- **Record expense payment**: select expense, enter amount/date; optional method/reference. An expense may be recorded as unpaid and paid in installments.
- **Finance summary**: show sales by `sold_on`, purchases by `purchased_on`, expenses by `spent_on`, cash receipts/payments by `payments.paid_on`, and current outstanding receivables/payables. Period filters apply to transaction totals and payments, not current balances. Do not label sales less purchases/expenses as profit because inventory cost-of-goods accounting is not implemented.

### Tasks and Activities

- **Create/edit task**: required title; optional description and due date. Status starts open.
- **Complete/reopen task**: one-click state transition; `completed_at` generated/cleared automatically.
- **Task list**: filter open, overdue, completed, due date. No user/assignee field in single-owner v1.

### Reports

- **Report filters**: required report type; optional date range and applicable animal/item/customer/supplier filters.
- **Reports**: livestock and poultry counts; production by item/source; inventory balances and movements; sales, purchases, expenses, payments and balances; health and breeding history; task completion.
- Report results are derived from source tables/views and exported from the client. No report snapshot or duplicated aggregate table.

## 4. Relationships and data flow

```text
auth.users 1--1 profiles
auth.users 1--N farms (farms.owner_id)
farms 1--N all farm-owned records (farm_id)

farms 1--N counterparties
counterparties 1--N sales (optional customer)
counterparties 1--N purchases (optional supplier)
counterparties 1--N expenses (optional supplier)

farms 1--N animals
animals 1--N health_records / breeding_events / production_records /
             inventory_consumptions / sale_lines
animals self-reference dam_id and sire_id

farms 1--N poultry_batches
poultry_batches 1--N poultry_batch_events / health_records /
                     production_records / inventory_consumptions / sale_lines
health_records 1--0..1 inventory_consumptions (treatment medicine use)

inventory_items 1--N purchase_lines / inventory_consumptions /
                     inventory_adjustments / production_records / sale_lines

purchases 1--N purchase_lines
sales 1--N sale_lines
sales, purchases, expenses 1--N payments (each payment targets exactly one)
```

- User ownership is established once at `farms.owner_id`; all business records carry `farm_id`. Do not copy `owner_id` into every operational row.
- Contact records are reused by foreign key. Customer/supplier names are not copied into documents; retain historical display via joins. If a counterparty name changes, the contact list reflects the new name.
- Inventory is a ledger calculated from receipt, production, adjustment, consumption, and product-sale records. It does not duplicate stock in a mutable balance column.
- Purchases increase stock through purchase lines; consumption decreases it; production increases it; adjustments change it; product sale lines decrease it.
- Individual animal sale lines are unique per animal via a partial unique index and make its derived status sold. Death health records make status dead. A database trigger locks the animal row and validates the current state before either a sale line or death record is inserted/updated; sold and dead are mutually exclusive terminal states. Normal app workflows do not delete or rewrite these terminal records.
- Poultry initial count plus count events less sold-bird quantities yields the current count. No separate current count is stored.
- A health record links to one animal or one batch. A breeding event links the dam and optional sire; offspring link back through animal parent IDs.
- Payments target exactly one sale, purchase, or expense. Balances are calculated; payment amounts are not copied onto the document.

## 5. Business rules

### Inventory receipts, consumption, and adjustments

1. A purchase is posted only when its required line items are saved. Posting a purchase line adds its quantity to stock.
2. Consumption subtracts its positive entered quantity from stock. It may optionally be attributed to an animal or poultry batch.
3. Production of an inventory product adds its quantity to stock.
4. An adjustment is a signed quantity change with a mandatory reason. It is not a substitute for recording a normal receipt or consumption.
5. Item stock is the sum of all applicable source records, shown in the item's base unit. If stock is below the optional reorder level, show a low-stock indicator.
6. Stock-changing forms use an atomic database transaction. Every path that can change stock locks all affected item rows in stable ID order, then recalculates the balance from the movement source rows after obtaining the locks; it must not rely on a client value, a cached view result, or a balance read taken before waiting for a lock. Reject a transaction if it is not running at the specified `READ COMMITTED` isolation level, unless an equivalent serializable/retry strategy is implemented and tested.
7. An operation that would make stock negative fails with a clear database error that the client maps to an actionable message. Do not clamp quantities or pretend the transaction succeeded.
8. Items with history cannot be deleted. Corrections use an edit with full revalidation or an explicit reversing adjustment; preserve the source history.
9. A database trigger rejects category or base-unit changes once any stock transaction or health-history row references the item. `name` and `reorder_level` remain editable. Item edits and all writes that create item references use the same item-row lock; after acquiring it, recheck history, active state, and stock using a fresh `READ COMMITTED` statement before committing.
10. Safe item correction: if the farm's unique-name rule blocks a replacement name, first rename the old item to a clearly marked retired name (including a short ID suffix), then create the correctly categorized/unitized replacement. Reconcile physical stock using explicit, reasoned adjustments on both items (recount rather than auto-convert if units differ), and retire the old item at zero stock. Keep historic transaction IDs, quantities, and prices unchanged; because item names are joined rather than snapshotted, historical screens show the retired label. Retired items remain queryable but cannot be used in new transactions. Do not rewrite historic quantities or relabel their units.

### Counterparty roles

- Sales require a counterparty role of `customer` or `both`; purchases and expenses require `supplier` or `both`.
- A database trigger rejects a role update that would make any existing sale, purchase, or expense reference invalid. Reference-validation triggers lock the counterparty row before accepting a new document reference; the role update holds the same row lock while checking existing documents. Changing `customer` to `both` or `supplier` to `both` is safe. Removing a role is allowed only if no document uses that role.
- The edit form disables role choices that conflict with existing references and explains which document type prevents the change. The user can keep `both`; do not silently reassign historical documents or create a duplicate contact to bypass the restriction.

### Sales and payments

- A sale requires one or more lines. A line must represent exactly one product, one individual animal, or one poultry batch.
- Product lines require product-category items and reduce inventory. Animal lines always represent one eligible animal. Poultry batch lines represent a positive integer number of birds and reduce the flock count.
- Sale line price is the price at sale time. Sale total is the sum of line quantity × unit price. A zero unit price is allowed for a documented no-charge transfer; user confirmation is recommended.
- Customer is optional for cash/anonymous sales. Payment is separate from the sale so partial, later, or multiple payments are supported.
- Payment target and farm must match. Payment amount is positive and cannot exceed the document's remaining balance. `record_payment` locks the target document row, then in a later `READ COMMITTED` statement recalculates both the document total and all committed payments before validating the new amount.
- Posted sales, lines, purchases, purchase lines, expenses, and payments have no direct client update/delete path in v1. A future audited correction operation must lock the affected document row, recalculate its total and paid amount after acquiring the lock, and preserve a valid balance; no such correction workflow is currently approved.
- Enforce one animal sale with `UNIQUE (farm_id,animal_id) WHERE animal_id IS NOT NULL`. A database trigger locks the animal row while validating both sale and death events, then re-reads both terminal-event sources in a separate `READ COMMITTED` statement after any lock wait. It rejects selling a dead animal and recording death for a sold animal. The partial unique death index prevents duplicate death events; the shared lock and fresh recheck serialize competing sale/death requests.
- The multi-line sale function locks all selected animal rows in stable ID order before validation and insertion. Database triggers repeat the fresh state check so direct or concurrent writes cannot bypass it.
- Reversals/refunds are not modeled in v1. Do not enter negative payments; flag the limitation and capture refunds in a future dedicated workflow.

### Purchases, suppliers, and payments

- Purchase lines represent received stock, never an unreceived order. Quantity is positive; unit cost is non-negative.
- Purchase total is the sum of quantity × unit cost. Supplier is optional for a cash-market purchase.
- Supplier payments are installments against a purchase. Payments cannot exceed the balance.
- Inventory purchases are not also entered as expenses. Non-stock operating outlays are expenses. This separation prevents duplicate cash-outflow reporting.
- Posted purchases, lines, and payments have no direct client update/delete path in v1. Any future audited correction must lock the target document and recheck totals and payments in the same transaction.
- **Approved livestock acquisition-cost exclusion:** v1 records only `acquired_on` for an animal/batch. It does not include `acquisition_type`, `purchase_cost`, or livestock acquisition-cost accounting. Consequently, livestock purchase outflows and related supplier payables are absent from Purchases, cash-paid/payables cards, and financial reports. `acquired_on` remains useful for husbandry but cannot distinguish born from purchased stock. No linked livestock acquisition/payment workflow or isolated cost field is part of v1.

- Multi-line purchase creation uses one atomic database function that requires at least one line. Clients cannot directly insert an empty purchase.

### Production and animal identification

- A production record links exactly one source animal or poultry batch and one product inventory item. Quantity is positive, date required, and source association is retained.
- Production increases product stock. Selling product then consumes that stock. Source attribution remains on production; product sale does not claim a particular production lot.
- Animal tags are optional but unique within a farm when entered. Unknown birth date/sex/parentage remain optional.
- Dam and sire must be different same-farm animals. A database trigger prevents parentage cycles. Sex mismatch is rejected only when sex is explicitly known; unknown sex is accepted.
- A sale or death changes derived animal status; status is not independently editable.
- Poultry count events must result in non-negative count. Sales and events are checked atomically against the batch.
- Every poultry sale and count event locks its parent batch row, then recalculates `initial_count + committed event changes - committed sold birds + pending change` in a separate fresh `READ COMMITTED` statement. It rejects a negative result. Multi-batch operations lock batch rows in stable ID order. A row lock alone is insufficient: never validate against a count calculated before a lock wait or from a stale client/view result.
- `initial_count` may be corrected only while the batch has no count events or bird sales. A database trigger serializes `initial_count` updates with event/sale writes using the same batch-row lock and rechecks for history after acquiring it. Once either type of history exists, reject any `initial_count` change; record corrections as adjustment events with a required reason. The edit form disables the starting-count field and directs the user to the adjustment-event form.

### Health and breeding

- Health and breeding history is append-first. Correcting a mistake requires an explicit edit permission and audit-appropriate UI; do not silently overwrite history.
- Every treatment submission, including treatment without medicine use, sends a client-generated UUID `request_id` to the atomic `record_treatment` function. A unique `(farm_id,request_id)` constraint makes retries safe: an identical payload (same target animal/batch, event type, date, description, medicine item, administered quantity, and notes) returns the original health record and optional linked consumption without another inventory deduction, while changed payload details using the same key are rejected. Check farm ownership before returning any prior result. A different key creates a separate treatment, including another same-day administration of the same medicine to the same animal or batch. When medicine and positive quantity are supplied together, the function creates exactly one linked `inventory_consumptions` row in the same transaction. The consumed quantity is stored only on that row; inventory views count it once. The UI captures medicine and quantity together and submits once.
- Expected due date is optional and is not an authoritative pregnancy outcome. Pregnancy check result is a separate event.

### Finance and dashboard

Show clearly labeled values, not an unqualified “profit”:

- **Sales recorded**: sale totals dated in the selected period.
- **Purchases recorded**: purchase totals dated in the selected period.
- **Operating expenses recorded**: expense amounts dated in the selected period.
- **Cash received**: payments linked to sales dated in the selected period.
- **Cash paid**: payments linked to purchases and expenses dated in the selected period.
- **Receivables**: sale totals less all sale payments through the current date.
- **Payables**: purchase and expense totals less linked payments through the current date.
- **Net cash movement**: cash received less cash paid for the selected period.

Sales, purchases, and expenses use their document transaction dates (`sold_on`, `purchased_on`, `spent_on`). Cash received and cash paid use `payments.paid_on`. Receivables and payables are current outstanding balances across all dates and are not changed by a dashboard period filter. Date ranges use the farm timezone for date-range display and all amounts use the farm currency. Sales less inventory purchases is not “net profit”; inventory valuation, cost of goods sold, depreciation, taxes, and livestock asset accounting are intentionally out of scope.

## 6. Security and Supabase implementation

### Authentication and profile creation

- Use Supabase Auth for sign-up, sign-in, session refresh, password reset, and email verification.
- On `auth.users` insert, a narrowly scoped trigger inserts `profiles(id)` and leaves `display_name` null. The trigger must be `SECURITY DEFINER`, set a fixed safe `search_path`, use no caller-provided dynamic SQL, and have only the permissions needed to insert that profile.
- Do not copy auth email into `profiles`; retrieve it from the authenticated session.
- Farm creation is an authenticated client operation. The database sets/checks `owner_id = auth.uid()`.
- Never put a Supabase service-role key in Vite environment variables, browser bundles, committed files, or client configuration. The client uses only the project's anon/publishable key.

### RLS policy model

Enable RLS on `profiles` and every public application table.

- `profiles`: select/update only where `id = auth.uid()`; insert only via the auth trigger. Prevent changing `id`.
- `farms`: select/update/delete only where `owner_id = auth.uid()`; insert only when `owner_id = auth.uid()`. Prevent owner transfer in v1.
- Every farm-owned table: select/insert/update/delete only if an `EXISTS` check finds its `farm_id` in `farms` with `owner_id = auth.uid()`. For update, enforce the check in both `USING` and `WITH CHECK`. Database triggers also reject inactive-item references and invalid category/unit transitions; hiding inactive items in a selector is not sufficient enforcement.
- Validate every referenced row belongs to the same farm using composite foreign keys, not RLS alone.
- Direct client inserts may not set `health_record_id`; direct updates may target only unlinked consumption rows and must leave the link null. Linked treatment-consumption rows are immutable to clients. Treatment medicine consumption is created only through `record_treatment`, which links it atomically to the health record. Matching medicine, source, and date do not by themselves make a separate administration a duplicate.
- For counterparty references, validate the role required by the workflow (customer/supplier/both) in a database constraint/trigger or controlled function; UI filtering is not a security control.
- Do not allow direct client writes to derived views. Grant only the required table/view/function privileges to `authenticated`.
- Anonymous users receive no access to farm data.

Policies should be explicit per table and tested with two authenticated test users and multiple farms. A request using a valid row ID from a different owner must return no row or a policy/constraint error, never its data.

### Database functions and transaction safety

- Prefer ordinary RLS-protected table operations for single-row edits.
- Implement multi-line sale/purchase creation, payment application, and treatment-plus-medicine recording as PostgreSQL functions invoked through Supabase RPC so each operation is atomic. Functions must validate `auth.uid()`, farm ownership, same-farm references, line requirements, and amounts.
- Enforce at least one line on each sale and purchase with deferred constraint triggers checked at commit, in addition to the RPC validation. The parent and lines therefore succeed or fail together, and no empty document can persist.
- Functions that do not need elevated privileges must be `SECURITY INVOKER`. If a `SECURITY DEFINER` function is unavoidable, pin `search_path`, schema-qualify objects, validate caller ownership inside the function, revoke `EXECUTE` from `PUBLIC`/`anon`, and grant only to `authenticated`.
- Use `READ COMMITTED` isolation for database operations that validate ledger totals or state derived from multiple rows. Triggers/RPCs must reject a higher isolation level unless an equivalent serialization-and-retry design is implemented and tested. Implement post-lock re-reads in a subsequent statement of a `VOLATILE` function, not a `STABLE` helper, so the query uses a fresh `READ COMMITTED` snapshot. A caller-supplied value or pre-lock aggregate is never authoritative.
- Enforce stock, animal, poultry-count, counterparty-role, item-category/unit, and payment invariants in the database, not just React validation. Lock all affected parent rows in stable ID order. After any wait for a lock, use a subsequent SQL statement to re-read the relevant source rows and recalculate the balance/state using a fresh `READ COMMITTED` snapshot before inserting or updating. Keep validation and the corresponding write in the same transaction. Row locks alone do not prove the check used a current aggregate.
- Apply this protocol to all inventory movements and item category/unit/retirement changes (lock item rows and recalculate stock/history), poultry events/sales/`initial_count` changes (lock batch rows and recalculate counts/history), animal sales/deaths (lock animal rows and re-read both terminal event sources), and payments (lock the target document and re-read its total and committed payment sum). Multi-key operations acquire locks in stable sorted order. Database checks and unique indexes remain final safeguards.
- Posted sale, purchase, expense, and payment records cannot be directly updated or deleted by clients in v1. Any future audited correction operation must take the same target-document lock and revalidate the resulting balance.
- The treatment RPC requires a client-generated UUID for every treatment submission and enforces uniqueness on `(farm_id,request_id)`. Identical retries return the original saved result; changed payloads using the same key are rejected; different keys permit distinct same-day administrations. Health record creation and optional inventory consumption stay atomic, with the same item lock and fresh stock check protecting each deduction.

- Return explicit errors for invalid state and map known database constraint/function errors to user-friendly form messages. Never substitute success-shaped defaults.
- No edge function or application server is required for v1. If later added, it must use the service-role key only server-side and must still enforce authorization.

## 8. Validation, migrations, and testing

### Validation

- Use React Hook Form with Zod (or the established equivalent if selected before implementation) for client-side required fields, numeric bounds, conditional line types, and conditional form fields.
- Normalize trimmed text and date-only values consistently. Validate currency and timezone during farm setup.
- Use server/database constraints as final authority for uniqueness, same-farm ownership, parentage, stock, balances, and conditional relationships.
- Show field-level messages for form input errors and a visible form-level message for authorization, network, and transaction errors.
- Do not disable optional fields from saving unless a dependent workflow truly requires them.

### Migration organization

Use ordered, reviewed SQL migrations under `supabase/migrations/`, for example:

1. `*_extensions_and_helpers.sql` — only required extensions and timestamp/ownership helpers.
2. `*_identity_and_farms.sql` — profile trigger, farms, RLS.
3. `*_contacts_and_animals.sql` — counterparties, livestock, poultry, health, breeding.
4. `*_inventory_and_production.sql` — items and source transactions; stock views/triggers.
5. `*_sales_purchases_finance.sql` — sales, purchases, expenses, payments; balance rules.
6. `*_tasks_and_reporting.sql` — tasks and read-only views/indexes.
7. `*_rls_review.sql` — final policy/privilege verification if policies were not colocated.

The exact migration count may be lower; do not create empty migrations merely to match this list. Each migration must be transactional where supported, reviewed, and tested against a local Supabase instance. Do not edit an already-applied migration; add a forward migration.

### Required quality gates

- Migration applies cleanly to an empty local database.
- Constraints reject cross-farm foreign keys, duplicate farm tags, invalid sale line variants, invalid payment targets, and negative quantities.
- RLS integration tests cover owner access, other-owner denial, and anonymous denial for every table.
- Concurrency tests use separate database sessions and a synchronization barrier (or controlled lock wait) to make transactions overlap; serial sequential requests are not accepted as concurrency tests. Exercise two stock movements that together exceed available inventory, simultaneous poultry sale/count writes, simultaneous payments exceeding a document balance, competing animal sale/death transitions, and item reclassification/retirement racing a referencing stock operation. Verify a fresh post-lock recalculation, a clear rejection or serialization error for the losing operation, and no negative/stale final balance.
- Race `initial_count` edits against the first poultry event and first batch sale. If the edit locks first, it may commit and the event/sale must use the new starting count; if the event/sale locks first, the starting-count edit must fail. After history exists, every later `initial_count` edit must fail and an adjustment event must be used.
- Constraint tests cover every table's required same-farm foreign keys, composite references, checks, and unique indexes listed in Section 2.3; role changes and inventory item retirement/correction are also tested.
- Form tests cover optional-field omission, conditional fields, decimal precision, and surfaced database errors.
- Treatment tests verify (1) different request UUIDs allow repeated same-day administrations of the same medicine to the same animal/batch, (2) identical-request retries return the original result without duplicate health records or inventory deductions, (3) conflicting-payload retries using one UUID are rejected, and (4) insufficient inventory leaves neither the health record nor consumption committed. Also verify one atomic health record and linked consumption, no movement without stock use, and at most one linked consumption per treatment. View tests reconcile inventory, poultry counts, document totals, and payment balances to their source rows.

## 10. Explicit v1 exclusions

The following are intentionally not proposed: multi-user farm teams, animal locations, custom fields, file attachments, feed formulas/rations, medication dosage/withdrawal tracking, vaccination schedules, breeding alerts, purchase orders, tax/VAT, multiple currencies per farm, unit conversions, inventory lots/expiry dates, livestock acquisition type/cost/payables, refunds, accounting-grade profit/cost-of-goods-sold, and offline sync.

Revisit an exclusion only when a specific operational workflow and its required form, relationships, and security rules are defined. Do not add a column “for future use.”

## 11. Approved v1 scope decision

V1 excludes livestock `acquisition_type`, `purchase_cost`, and livestock acquisition-cost accounting. This decision is approved, not pending. The schema, forms, migrations, reports, and finance calculations must not add these fields or represent livestock acquisition costs. The consequence is that livestock acquisition outflows and related supplier payables are absent from purchasing, cash, and financial reports. Reconsider only through a separately approved future scope and a linked acquisition/payment workflow; do not add isolated cost fields.
